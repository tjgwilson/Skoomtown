Skoomtown is a community & a party, a place we want everybody to be able to relax, have a ridiculous amount of fun, and feel safe while they’re at it.

We expect everyone to help keep up the following values by embodying them and learning from any mistakes made along the way.

Respect & Inclusivity

We respect everyone, and in-case it’s not obvious enough; regardless of their ethnicity, culture, religion, gender, sexuality, body, disability, relationship type, appearance, etc. However, our respect does not extend to people who are knowingly perpetuating harmful behaviour.

Respecting chosen names, pronouns, and gender identities is non-negotiable at Skoomtown.

Boundaries & Autonomy

Everyone gets to decide what they’re comfortable with – respect everyone’s right to say no, change their mind, leave a conversation, or simply not engage. This can be tricky but is especially important around romantic and sexual attention. Flirt away, as long as it is consensual and reciprocated.

Do not expect conversation, flirting, physical touch or anything else from someone, regardless of how they are dressed, how drunk they might be, or how they might be behaving.

When offering intimate comments, flirting, or physical touch, be mindful that the other person might be uncomfortable and struggling to express themselves. If they are not enthusiastically reciprocating or independently offering the same kind of attention towards you, they might be uncomfortable. It is always extremely cool to check rather than relying on your own assumptions, especially if you're intoxicated.

Accountability

When we get something wrong, we’re expected to hear the feedback, reflect on it, and make a genuine effort to do better. We don’t expect people to drop to their knees in agonising repentance, but we do expect everyone to actively engage honestly and respectfully in constructive criticism when it’s brought to their attention.

Community

As much as we love our big commercial festivals, this isn’t one. It’s a community, a group of friends in a field having a good time and looking after each other.

Look out for anyone who needs help, respect other people’s belongings, speak to an organiser if you see anything that doesn’t look quite right.

Take responsibility for your rubbish – sort waste from recycling so that the organisers don’t have to do it for you at 5pm on Monday. Respect the land that we’re lucky enough to party on and leave the site in good condition. Leave no trace and all that jazz.

If you find someone else’s belongings, especially something important like medication, don’t pocket it or throw it away – return it to lost property or hand it in to an organiser so it can find its way back to its thankful owner.

We don't have to be perfect, but we do have to be willing to be respectful, listen, and take responsibility for our impact on other people.
